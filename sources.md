# Sources (verified 2026-11-04)

## 1. ACSM Position Stand — Progression Models in Resistance Training for Healthy Adults
Med Sci Sports Exerc. 2009;41(3):687-708.
https://journals.lww.com/acsm-msse/Fulltext/2009/03000/Progression_Models_in_Resistance_Training_for.26.aspx

Verbatim (summary): "it is recommended that 2-10% increase in load be applied when the
individual can perform the current workload for one to two repetitions over the desired
number." Also: novice 8-12 RM; hypertrophy emphasis on 6-12 RM zone; multiple-set
programs recommended for maximizing hypertrophy.

On-screen use: cards "PROGRESSIVE OVERLOAD" and "THE 2-10% RULE".

## 2. Lopez P, et al. — Resistance Training Load Effects on Muscle Hypertrophy and Strength Gain: Systematic Review and Network Meta-analysis
Med Sci Sports Exerc. 2021;53(6):1206-1216.
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8126497/ (PMID 33433148)

Verbatim (abstract): 28 studies, 747 healthy adults, all sets to volitional failure.
"no differences in muscle hypertrophy between RT loads were found in overall
(P = 0.113-0.469)". "Muscle strength improvement was superior for both high-load and
moderate-load compared with low-load RT (SMD, 0.60-0.63 and 0.34-0.35, respectively)".
High-load vs low-load strength: SMD 0.60, 95% CI 0.38-0.82 (P < 0.001).

On-screen use: cards "STRENGTH FAVORS HEAVY" and "HYPERTROPHY: EITHER LOAD".

## 3. Schoenfeld BJ, Ogborn D, Krieger JW — Dose-response relationship between weekly resistance training volume and increases in muscle mass
J Sports Sci. 2017;35(11):1073-1082.
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27433992/

Verbatim (abstract): "Outcomes for weekly sets as a continuous variable showed a
significant effect of volume on changes in muscle size (P = 0.002)." "a graded
dose-response relationship whereby increases in RT volume produce greater gains in
muscle hypertrophy."

On-screen use: outro "Bonus" line only. The 10+ sets/muscle/week categorical
comparison was a trend (P = 0.074), so it is not stated as a finding.
